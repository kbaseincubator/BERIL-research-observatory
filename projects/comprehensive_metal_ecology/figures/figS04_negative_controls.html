Figure S4. Named negative controls: universal housekeeping gene sets show similar associations.

PGLS β and 95% CI (±1.96 SE) for three named negative control gene sets (ribosomal proteins, amino acid biosynthesis, DNA repair) compared to the primary metal-gene target (β = −0.021). All three controls are significant, reflecting the genome-wide streamlining signal present in universal genes. The metal signal does not substantially exceed these controls at the whole-set level; the key distinction emerges internally (cofactor vs resistance split, Figure 3).
Interpretation note: Named negative controls are expected to also show a streamlining signal because genome-wide gene density correlates with niche breadth. The relevant comparison is whether metal genes show a distinctive pattern (cofactor/resistance split) rather than a larger overall effect. See Figure 3 for the internal split permutation.
