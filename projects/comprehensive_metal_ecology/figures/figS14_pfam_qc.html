Figure S14. Pfam metal-domain audit: 92.9% of primary KOs lack Pfam metal-clan evidence, supporting KEGG-curated gene selection.

Left: Evidence summary for 140 primary KOs — only 10 have a known metal clan (PF) or metal singleton Pfam domain. Right: Category breakdown by functional group. The predominance of Transport/Homeostasis (n = 106/140) reflects the breadth of metal-handling functions beyond structural metal-binding domains. Absence of Pfam evidence does not indicate non-metal function; most ABC-type and P-type ATPase transporters are functionally validated by KEGG biochemistry but lack metal-specific Pfam annotations.
(A) Pfam metal-domain evidence (n = 140 KOs)
Has metal Pfam (n=10, 7.1%)
No Pfam evidence (n=130, 92.9%)
(B) Category breakdown (n = 140 KOs)
Interpretation: The 92.9% absence of Pfam metal-clan evidence is expected and does not indicate false positives in the gene list. Metal-ion transport (ABC transporters, P-type ATPases), sensing (two-component regulators), and cofactor biosynthesis (vitamin B12, molybdopterin, Fe–S clusters) are functionally well-characterised by KEGG but lack dedicated Pfam metal-binding clans. A Pfam-based gene list would recover only 7% of the curated KO set and would be substantially underpowered for this analysis. KEGG functional annotation is more appropriate for cataloguing these diverse metal-handling roles.
