# Review: Metagenomic Environment Prediction

## Summary

This project asks a well-posed, falsifiable question — does MAG-level metal-gene density predict local metal mobility, and does the genus-level P1 signal (`comprehensive_metal_ecology`) survive at individual-genome resolution? — and answers it honestly with a negative result, backed by two independent datasets (SPIRE, 15,957 MAGs; MGnify, 8,849 MAGs), pre-registered pass/fail thresholds, spatial block cross-validation, SHAP-based mechanism interrogation, and a geographic holdout. All headline numbers in REPORT.md were traced back to their underlying data files (`cv_results.csv`, `holdout_results.json`, `pgls_validation_results.csv`) and matched. The main weaknesses are project-hygiene rather than scientific: two fully executed, informative notebooks (NB06, NB07) that materially bear on the reliability of the H5 niche-breadth construct are absent from README.md, RESEARCH_PLAN.md, and REPORT.md entirely; the README's status table and execution-order section are stale relative to what was actually run; three of the earlier notebooks' figures don't follow the repo's mandatory figure-style module; and RESEARCH_PLAN.md contains an internal contradiction about which metal targets exist in the data. None of these undermine the reported H1–H5 conclusions, but they represent real gaps in the write-up a downstream reader would trip over.

## Methodology

The research question and five pre-registered hypotheses (H1–H5) are stated with explicit, falsifiable pass/fail criteria (RESEARCH_PLAN.md lines 19–42), and the plan explicitly commits to reporting negative results as informative (lines 100–102) — this commitment is honored in REPORT.md. Data sources are clearly identified (SPIRE download endpoints, `kescience_mgnify` Spark tables, `arkinlab.envdbs.csu_metal_mobility_grid`, `arkinlab.envdbs.soilgrids_master`) and cross-referenced to the parent project's curated KO set. The spatial block CV design (k-means k=5 on lat/lon, fixed seed) and the pre-specified Australia holdout with a 1.25× RMSE-ratio threshold are sound choices for guarding against spatial autocorrelation leakage.

Two methodology issues:

1. **Internal contradiction in the pre-specified target list.** RESEARCH_PLAN.md's H1 (line 20) states the test requires support "for ≥2 of the 5 targets (Cu, Zn, Pb, Cr, Ni)," but the "Pre-specified decisions → Primary target" section (lines 86–87) states the CSU grid provides only 6 targets — Cu, As, Cd, Cr, Hg, Pb — and explicitly notes "Zn and Ni not in the CSU grid." Two of the five metals named in H1's own success criterion (Zn, Ni) are stated elsewhere in the same document to not exist in the data. This should be reconciled — presumably H1's target list is a leftover from an earlier draft.
2. **Multi-target hypotheses tested on one target.** H1/H2/H3 as pre-specified require evidence from ≥2 of the available metal targets, but the executed analysis (NB02, NB02b) only ran the CV/SHAP pipeline for PF1_Cu. This is disclosed as Limitation #1 in REPORT.md, which is good practice, but it means the literal pre-registered criterion for H1/H2/H3 could not be evaluated as written — the reported "NOT SUPPORTED" verdicts are single-metal findings being reported against a multi-metal-designed test. Worth rephrasing the hypothesis outcome language (e.g., "NOT SUPPORTED on the tested target (Cu); untested for As/Cd/Cr/Hg/Pb") rather than an unqualified verdict.

Reproducibility is otherwise good: the pipeline is documented step-by-step, notebooks are numbered and organized setup → query → analysis → visualization, and there's no `requirements.txt`, which would help a fresh environment reproduce results (missing, not a blocker given BERDL/JupyterHub is the assumed execution environment). There is no explicit `## Reproduction` section in either README.md or REPORT.md describing expected runtimes or which notebooks need Spark vs. run locally from cache — README's "Execution order" section covers ordering but not runtime/Spark-dependency, and it is stale (see Code Quality).

## Findings Assessment

- **H1–H4 conclusions are well supported by the data shown**, and the geographic-transfer caveat in H4 (negative R² despite passing the RMSE-ratio threshold) is exactly the kind of self-critical check that should be applauded — the report explicitly warns readers not to over-interpret "SUPPORTED" as "the model works" (REPORT.md lines 37, 137).
- **H5's "Discoveries" claim is reasonably scoped but sits on thinner evidence than the framing suggests, and a directly relevant robustness check is missing from the writeup.** The Discoveries entry states the P1 signal "is sign-concordant but non-significant at MAG resolution across two independent datasets" and frames this as establishing "an ecological resolution bound." The underlying evidence is two non-significant PGLS fits (SPIRE p=0.22, MGnify p=0.25) that happen to share a sign with P1's significant β — this is a real and worth-reporting observation, but three same-signed coefficients with wide, overlapping confidence intervals and non-significant p-values is a weak basis for the stronger claim "operates at ≥genus level, not at the isolate/MAG level." A more precise framing would be: "consistent with, but not independently confirming, a genus-level-only effect."
- **NB06/NB07 contain a directly relevant confound finding that is not surfaced anywhere.** NB06 constructs an alternative, genome-sampling-derived Levins' B (from SPIRE MAG biome distributions, independent of the MicrobeAtlas 16S measure used in H5/NB04) and finds it phylogenetically much weaker than P1's measure (λ=0.20 vs. P1's λ≈0.81, per NB07's cell 6 comment). NB07 then shows this genome-derived niche breadth is almost entirely explained by study/biome sampling intensity, not biology: Pearson r=0.80 (p≈2×10⁻¹⁴⁵) against log(number of unique studies sampling a genus), R²=0.71 in a simple OLS. Once study count is included as a covariate, the KO-density β **flips sign** (from −0.056, p=0.41 to +0.018, p=0.57) and the PGLS phylogenetic signal collapses to λ≈0.0001; a rarefied analysis (1 MAG per genus-study cell) similarly flips the sign (+0.042, p=0.71). This is a genuinely useful negative/cautionary result — it shows that a plausible alternative construction of "niche breadth" from MAG sampling data is a sampling-effort artifact, not ecology — but it is invisible to a reader of README.md/REPORT.md. It should be written up (even briefly) in REPORT.md's Limitations or Discoveries section, since it directly informs how much weight to put on any future genus-level niche-breadth construction derived from assembly/sampling counts rather than curated occurrence data. It is also a strong candidate for `memories/pitfalls.md` / the pitfall-capture workflow, since "genus-level niche-breadth proxies built from MAG/study sampling counts are confounded by sequencing effort (r≈0.8, λ collapses to ~0)" is exactly the kind of gotcha likely to recur in other BERDL ecology projects using SPIRE- or MGnify-derived diversity metrics.
- **Limitations section is thorough and specific** (7 itemized limitations covering single-metal scope, MAG quality filtering, holdout sparsity, download completeness, annotation-pipeline differences, an unevaluated hypothesis, and target non-comparability). This is a real strength of the write-up.
- No analysis is left as "to be filled" — every notebook referenced in REPORT.md's Supporting Evidence table has a corresponding executed notebook and output file (NB06/NB07 are the exception, being complete but unreferenced rather than incomplete).
