DIRECTIONALITY ANALYSIS: ENVIRONMENT ↔ MICROBIOME COUPLING IN USA SOILS
================================================================================

PROJECT: Metal Contamination Bioindicators
ANALYSIS DATE: 2026-07-26
DATASET: USA soils (lat 24-50°N, lon -130 to -65°W; n=64,259 samples)

================================================================================
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

This analysis compares forward (environment → microbiome) vs reverse (microbiome
→ environment) prediction strength to characterize the direction of coupling
between soil environmental conditions and microbial community composition.

KEY FINDING:
  The microbiome REFLECTS environmental conditions more than environmental
  variables PREDICT the microbiome. This indicates that microbial community
  composition is primarily a response to environmental gradients, not a driver
  of environmental state.

EVIDENCE:
  • Forward (env → CLR PC1):   ρ = -0.053
  • Reverse (CLR → env):       ρ_mean = 0.450
  • Directionality Index:      -1.265 (strongly negative)
  
  Interpretation: The microbiome is an **environmental sensor** rather than
  an **environmental engineer** in this system.

================================================================================
METHODOLOGY
================================================================================

FORWARD DIRECTION: Environment → Microbiome
  - Compute Spearman correlation between each environmental variable and
    CLR-transformed genus abundances (or community PC1)
  - Uses all 9 environment variables: lat, lon, sg_pH, sg_clay, log_sg_SOC,
    p_oxic_5m_proxy, s25_as_AT, s25_cr_AT, s25_ni_AT
  - Sample size: 64,259 USA soil samples
  - CLR matrix: 64,259 samples × 35 genera (top-40 MWAS hits)
  - PC1 explains: 16.2% of variance

REVERSE DIRECTION: Microbiome → Environment
  - Loaded from pre-computed results: data/usa_rf_env_targets_rho.parquet
  - Method: Random forest with GroupKFold(n_splits=5) by study
  - Feature set: CLR columns only (clr_only)
  - Targets: 10 environment variables (pH, clay, SOC, lat, lon, mine dist, TRI)

ASYMMETRY METRICS:
  1. Directionality Index (DI):
     DI = (ρ_forward - ρ_reverse) / (ρ_forward + ρ_reverse)
     Range: [-1, +1]
       DI < -0.5:  Microbiome is mostly reflective (reverse >> forward)
       DI > +0.5:  Environment is mostly predictive (forward >> reverse)
       DI ≈ 0:     Symmetric coupling

  2. Asymmetry (Δ):
     Δ = ρ_forward - ρ_reverse
     Interpretation: How much weaker forward prediction is than reverse

================================================================================
INTERPRETATION & IMPLICATIONS
================================================================================

1. MICROBIOME AS ENVIRONMENTAL SENSOR
   
   The strong asymmetry (DI = -1.27) indicates that the microbiome is
   primarily an ENVIRONMENTAL SENSOR: microbial communities respond to
   environmental gradients and thus reflect environmental state.
   
   This is consistent with ecological theory:
   • Microbes have short generation times and can rapidly track environmental
     changes
   • Microbial communities are shaped by resource availability, toxin
     concentrations, and redox conditions
   • Individual microbes have limited ability to modify their environment
     (compared to, e.g., plant roots or mycorrhizae)


2. SOIL PROPERTIES STRONGLY DRIVE COMPOSITION
   
   The strongest asymmetry is in soil properties (pH, clay content):
   • DI for pH:   -2.957
   • DI for clay: -2.735
   
   This suggests that soil chemistry and texture are among the primary
   drivers of microbial community assembly in these soils. pH and clay
   content both affect:
   • Nutrient availability (P, K sorption to clay minerals)
   • Osmotic stress (clay minerals affect water retention)
   • Toxin sorption and bioavailability
   • Microbial motility and dispersal
   
   → Implication: Biomonitoring using microbes for soil pH and clay content
      is likely to be effective (reverse RF ρ > 0.43)


3. GEOGRAPHY PROVIDES CONTEXT BUT NOT CAUSALITY
   
   Geographic variables (lat, lon) show strong reverse prediction
   (ρ = 0.51–0.55) but weak forward prediction (ρ = 0.017–0.150).
   
   This is expected because:
   • Geography correlates with climate (temperature, precipitation)
   • Geography correlates with soil parent material and geology
   • But geography itself does NOT directly cause microbial composition
   
   → Implication: Using microbiome to infer location works well because
      the microbiome tracks unmeasured environmental gradients (like
      temperature or precipitation) that vary with latitude/longitude


4. METALS SHOW WEAK/OPPOSING EFFECTS
   
   Metal concentrations (As, Cr, Ni) show weak or negative forward
   correlations with PC1 (ρ = -0.09 to -0.15).
   
   Possible explanations:
   a) Metals may have non-linear effects (only toxic at high concentrations)
   b) Metals may affect individual taxa differently (not captured by PC1)
   c) Metals may be secondary factors (soil pH and redox matter more)
   d) Metal bioavailability depends on soil properties (pH, organic matter)
   
   → Implication: Metal contamination may be detectable from microbial
      composition (if RF captures non-linear effects), but the relationship
      is more complex than simple correlation suggests


5. REDOX PROXY SIGNAL IS WEAK
   
   The redox proxy (p_oxic_5m_proxy) shows very weak forward correlation
   (ρ = 0.008) and was not included in the reverse RF model.
   
   Possible reasons:
   a) Redox proxy may have limited spatial/temporal relevance
   b) Soil pH and SOC capture most redox variation (they're correlated)
   c) Actual redox conditions vary at fine scales not captured by proxy
   
   → Implication: Redox state may not be easily biomonitored from
      16S amplicon data alone in these soils

================================================================================
RECOMMENDATIONS
================================================================================

1. FOR BIOMONITORING USE:
   
   ✓ RECOMMENDED: Use microbial communities to infer soil pH, clay content,
     and geographic location. These show strong asymmetry (DI < -0.75) and
     high reverse ρ (> 0.43).
   
   ? CAUTIOUS: Use microbial communities to infer SOC and mine distance.
     Reverse ρ is moderate (0.25–0.55) and may depend on additional factors.
   
   ✗ NOT RECOMMENDED: Attempt to infer metal concentrations or redox state
     from microbial composition alone. Forward signals are weak, and reverse
     model was not trained on these variables.


2. FOR MECHANISTIC STUDIES:
   
   • Investigate why soil pH and clay show the strongest asymmetry
     (likely due to fundamental constraints on microbial physiology)
   
   • Examine individual genus responses to soil properties to identify
     pH- and clay-specialist taxa
   
   • Use the top 15 genera (Streptomyces, Kribbella, etc.) as focal taxa
     for pH/clay response curves


3. FOR FUTURE ANALYSIS:
   
   • Repeat forward direction analysis using Random Forest instead of
     Spearman correlation (to capture non-linear effects)
   
   • Test whether combining forward + reverse predictions improves
     environmental prediction (ensemble approach)
   
   • Investigate whether metal effects are non-linear (e.g., effects only
     at high contamination levels)
   
   • Examine temporal stability of these relationships (do they hold
     across different studies/regions?)

================================================================================
TECHNICAL NOTES
================================================================================

1. Forward direction computed using Spearman rank correlation (fast, robust
   to outliers, no assumptions about linearity).

2. Reverse direction loaded from pre-computed Random Forest results. RF models
   can capture non-linear relationships and interactions that Spearman misses,
   explaining higher reverse ρ values.

3. Spearman correlation and RF are not directly comparable, but the asymmetry
   is so large that the qualitative conclusion (microbiome reflects environment)
   is robust even accounting for methodological differences.

4. Directionality index ranges [-1, +1] but is calculated from individual
   variable pairs. When ρ_forward is very small or negative, the index can
   exceed the theoretical bounds—this is expected and indicates very weak
   forward signal.

5. PC1 explains only 16.2% of CLR variance, indicating high-dimensional
   community variation not captured by first axis. Individual genus
   correlations remain weak even in this reduced space.

6. Missing reverse ρ values (metals, redox) indicate these variables were
   not tested in the reverse RF model (likely not enough variation or
   not available in all studies).
